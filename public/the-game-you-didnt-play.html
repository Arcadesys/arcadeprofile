An unplayed adventure / A GM's open notebook

The Game
You Didn't Play.

There was supposed to be a tabletop game. There were hours of preparation. There were friends to invite. And then there was a disagreement about the tools used to make it.

This is the archive of Message in a Bottle, a planned Savage Worlds campaign. The intended destination is a full, unapologetically spoiler-filled account of the adventure that could have unfolded.

The work

One game master prepared a campaign intended to be played with other people. AI tools were part of that creative workflow. The author chose to develop it, made editorial decisions, and invested real time in doing so.

The thing that never happened

Because the game was not played, there is no canonical session log. No real player choices, discoveries, dice results, or endings exist to report. Planned paths must be described as possibilities, never as events.

03 / Behind the screen

An archive, not a scorecard.

The players get to decide whether they sit down. The maker gets to decide what tools are on the workbench.

This page does not identify or shame anyone who declined to participate. The point is to document a piece of human-directed creative labor that might otherwise be reduced to one adjective: AI-assisted.

Even a complete set of GM notes is not a replay of the game. A tabletop campaign is a blueprint for a collaborative event, and some of its best moments can only happen when other people arrive and do something unexpected.

What is documented

The title, system, approximate time spent preparing, the involvement of AI tools, and the fact that play did not occur.

What is not yet documented

The source-verified plot, named characters, encounters, branches, reveals, and endings. Those should be added from the GM's original materials before release.

04 / Editorial notes

What the record can say.

Did the players actually see these scenes?

No. The adventure was prepared, not played. Even after source notes are recovered, this page will describe what the GM planned, not pretend that a session occurred.

Who made the campaign?

Its human author developed it using AI-assisted tools. The publication should show the author's intention and decisions, and be candid about AI involvement.

Why not fill the missing scenes with plausible material?

Because “what would have happened” is an archival claim. A convincing invented ending would be fiction about the campaign, not evidence of the campaign that was built.

The Singularity Log / Companion essay

It wasn't a referendum on whether the work was worth making.

It was a disagreement about whether the tools used to make the work made it unacceptable in advance. Those are different questions.
